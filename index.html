🔍 What Is AppDNS?

Every phone has a setting called Private DNS. It decides which DNS server your phone talks to. A DNS server is the part of the internet that looks up the name of a website and finds the address behind it. Some DNS servers block ads and trackers. Others are better for general browsing. Normally you can only pick one of them for your whole phone.

AppDNS changes that. When you open an app, AppDNS checks the rule you created for that app and switches your Private DNS to the server you chose. When you open a different app, AppDNS switches again. Everything happens quietly in the background, so you do not have to touch any settings yourself.

Because AppDNS uses the Private DNS feature that is already built into Android, it does not need a VPN slot on your phone. Your internet traffic is not routed through any server run by AppDNS. Nothing is logged, nothing is collected, and the whole project is open source so anyone can check the code.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • Block ads inside one app only. Send your browser to an ad-blocking DNS server while your bank app keeps using a normal, fast server.
  • Use different DNS servers for different apps. Each app on your phone can follow its own rule.
  • Keep ad blockers out of apps that break. Some apps misbehave when ads are blocked. Give those apps a clean DNS server and leave the rest protected.
  • Turn on family protection when the kids grab the phone. Point games and video apps at a family-safe DNS server.
  • No VPN icon, no VPN slowdown. AppDNS uses Android's own Private DNS setting, so your connection is not tunnelled through an extra server.
  • Switch from the Quick Settings panel. A tile lets you flip the whole system on or off in one tap.

🔄 How the Switching Works

AppDNS watches for one thing only: which app is in the foreground. The moment you open an app that has a rule, AppDNS updates the Private DNS setting in Android to the server you picked. When you leave that app, AppDNS applies the rule that belongs to whatever you opened next, or returns to your default server.

There is no tunnel, no proxy, and no traffic going through a third party. Your requests go straight from your phone to the DNS server you selected, using DNS-over-TLS, which keeps those lookups encrypted.

If you ever want to stop everything at once, turn the main switch off. AppDNS hands your Private DNS setting back to Android and stops working until you turn it on again.

🧩 Quick Settings Tile

AppDNS includes a Quick Settings tile. Pull down your notification shade twice, tap the pencil or edit button, and drag the AppDNS tile into your active panel. After that, one tap from anywhere turns the switching behaviour on or off without opening the app.

The tile is handy when you want to pause DNS switching for a while, for example while troubleshooting a website that will not load, then turn it back on just as quickly.

🌐 DNS Servers You Can Use

AppDNS works with any DNS-over-TLS server that has a hostname. That includes popular options such as ad-blocking resolvers, family-safe resolvers, and general privacy resolvers. If you already use a managed DNS service, you can point an app rule at it.

The app stores your rules locally on your phone. Changing a rule takes effect the next time you open the app that rule belongs to.

🔒 Privacy and Open Source

AppDNS collects no data. It has no analytics, no advertising, and no account system. Your list of rules stays on your device. The app does not route your internet traffic, so there is nothing for it to see even if it wanted to.

The full source code is publicly available, so anyone can read it, check it, or build it themselves. If you spot something you would like to improve, you are welcome to open an issue on the repository or send in a change.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The switch will not stay on. Open your phone's app settings for AppDNS and make sure the permission to change Private DNS is granted. Some phones also require you to remove battery restrictions for the app.

An app loses its internet connection. The DNS server you picked for that app may not be reachable. Open AppDNS, edit that app's rule, and choose a different server.

A website will not load in the browser. That usually means the ad-blocking rule is blocking something the page needs. Switch that one app to a normal resolver.

Nothing seems to change. Some phones show the Private DNS value with a short delay. Leave the app and open it again, then check your phone's network settings to confirm the value updated.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does AppDNS need a VPN? No. It uses Android's built-in Private DNS setting, which is why no VPN connection is created and no VPN icon appears.

Does it slow down my internet? DNS lookups are tiny. AppDNS only changes a setting, it does not sit in the path of your traffic.

Will it work on any Android phone? AppDNS relies on the Private DNS feature, which is available on modern Android versions. The app follows the standard settings that Android provides.

What happens if I uninstall it? Your Private DNS setting stays at whatever value was last applied. You can change it back at any time from your phone's network settings.

Where do I get the newest version? Visit the AppDNS releases page to download the application. The most recent release is always listed first.
